1. Annotated Wikipedia Source Text

Lead Section

The history of Nintendo, a Japanese video game company based in Kyoto, starts in 1889 when Fusajiro Yamauchi founded "Yamauchi Nintendo", a producer of hanafuda playing cards. His successor Hiroshi Yamauchi had Nintendo producing toys and video games.

Shigeru Miyamoto designed Donkey Kong (1981) for arcades: Nintendo's first international hit game, and origin of the company's mascot, Mario. After the American video game crash of 1983, Nintendo released their Japanese Famicom home console as the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) in 1985. Miyamoto's innovative titles, Super Mario Bros. and The Legend of Zelda (1987), greatly influenced gaming. The Game Boy handheld console (1989) was extraordinarily successful, alongside the Pokémon media franchise.

In 2002, Satoru Iwata became president, leading development of the Nintendo DS handheld (2004) and the Wii home console (2006). Before dying in 2015, Iwata led development of the successful Nintendo Switch (2017). Today, Nintendo's historical heritage is preserved at the Nintendo Museum (opened in 2024) in Kyoto, dedicated to video game preservation.

1889–1949: Hanafuda cards

Nintendo was founded as Yamauchi Nintendo by Fusajiro Yamauchi on September 23, 1889. Based in Kyoto, Japan, the business produced and marketed hanafuda, a type of Japanese playing card. During the era of 1889–1929, the company expanded card production. In 1929, Fusajiro Yamauchi retired and allowed his adopted son-in-law to take over.

In the period of 1949–2002, under Hiroshi Yamauchi, Nintendo transformed from a playing card manufacturer into a global video game giant, before Satoru Iwata took leadership in 2002–2015.
