01

THE STARTING POINT

The Project

Nintendo Entertainment Heritage explores how Nintendo has developed different forms of entertainment, from traditional playing cards to video games and consoles. It connects products, people, characters and places to explore continuity and change in the company’s history.

Why I chose this topic

I chose Nintendo because I wanted to look beyond individual games and consoles and explore the connections between different forms of entertainment. Two connections shaped my selection: the relationship between Donkey Kong, Mario and the name Jumpman, and the comparison between Nintendo’s origins in Hanafuda cards and physical card play within the Pokémon franchise. These interests helped me define the project’s focus, select the entities and review how their relationships should be represented.

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

How can the connections between Nintendo’s products, people, characters and places help us understand continuity and change in its entertainment history?

PATH 01 / CHARACTER CONTINUITYHow do Donkey Kong and Mario Bros. connect through Mario and the name Jumpman?

PATH 02 / FORMS OF PLAYWhat does comparing Hanafuda and Pokémon Trading Card Game reveal about physical card play within Nintendo’s entertainment history?

The comparison between Hanafuda and Pokémon Trading Card Game is a thematic interpretation. It does not imply that Pokémon cards developed directly from Hanafuda.

From interpretation to structured data

The project uses 22 main entities, supported by additional records for names, categories, places and events. Selected historical passages are annotated using TEI, a standard for encoding texts in XML. The catalogue and its relationships are then exported as RDF: statements that connect resources through clearly identified properties. The models and visualizations allow readers to examine these connections, consult the evidence and understand the choices behind the project.

02

DEFINING THE SCOPE

Domain Analysis and Source Selection

The main source is the English Wikipedia article History of Nintendo. The project uses selected passages that document the chosen entities, provide essential context and support the two research paths.

Retained passages preserve their original wording. Omissions are marked with <gap reason="sampling"/> and explained in the teiHeader, which also identifies the Wikipedia revision used. Only references cited in the selected passages are reproduced, keeping their original numbering.

Additional sources, including publications by Nintendo and The Pokémon Company, support details such as the name Jumpman and the regional launches of Pokémon TCG. This information is documented in the catalogue, separately from the Wikipedia transcription.

Twenty of the 22 main entities have annotated mentions in the selected text. Arcade cabinet is included as a contextual category, while Video game preservation is a research concept supported by additional sources. The model distinguishes characters from names, franchises from individual games, and console models from physical objects.

Twelve supporting records make the relationships more precise. They include the name Jumpman; Uji, Japan and the United States; three categories describing cards and card games; four events covering Nintendo’s business origins, the museum opening and the regional card-game launches; and the project document itself.

These records support the 22 main entities. They help distinguish names, places, categories and events without expanding the main selection.

04

CONNECTIONS IN ORDINARY LANGUAGE

Theoretical Model

The theoretical model describes the selected resources and their connections in ordinary language. It provides the basis for the conceptual model and the RDF dataset.

One overview brings the main connections together. Three detail diagrams focus on people and products, character continuity, and forms of card play.

People, Games and Console Models

People connect Nintendo’s products across different periods. The model records selected contributions to game works and console development, as well as employment relationships with Nintendo. It also connects the console models to their manufacturer.

These relationships distinguish the organisation from the people involved. A contribution does not imply that one person created a product alone.

Mario across Donkey Kong and Mario Bros.

Donkey Kong (1981) and Mario Bros. (1983) feature the same character, Mario. Jumpman is represented as an appellation of Mario, with Donkey Kong providing its documented context.

This distinction allows the model to express continuity across the games without creating a second character for the name Jumpman.

From Hanafuda to Pokémon TCG

Hanafuda and Pokémon Trading Card Game represent different forms of physical card play. The model connects them through categories: Hanafuda belongs to Playing cards, while Pokémon TCG is classified as a Collectible card game, a narrower category of Card game. Playing cards and Card game are related concepts.

Pokémon TCG is also connected to the wider Pokémon franchise. Together, these relationships support the thematic comparison introduced in the research question.

Overview

The overview places these paths within a wider historical and geographical context. It includes Nintendo’s business origins, selected events and places, and the museum site in Uji. Video game preservation is represented as a subject of the project, rather than as an activity automatically attributed to the museum.

FROM IDEAS TO A SHARED VOCABULARY

Conceptual Model

The conceptual model translates the relationships described in ordinary language into classes and properties from shared vocabularies. A class identifies a category of resource, while a property expresses a relationship.

The diagrams were created in yEd using the Graffoo palette. One overview presents the model, while three detail diagrams focus on the main research paths. They show classes; the concrete resources and their connections appear in the RDF visualizations.

Reusing Existing Vocabularies

Vocabulary Use in the project
CIDOC CRM People, organisation, places, activities, appellations and conceptual objects
FRBRoo Video games represented as works
Schema.org Console models, the card game, events and selected relationships
SKOS Categories and relationships between concepts
Dublin Core Terms Contributions, subjects and other descriptive relationships

Some choices require a distinction between a resource and its representation. Consoles are commercial models rather than individual physical specimens. Jumpman is an appellation of Mario. The Nintendo Museum record represents the site, while preservation is a subject explored by the project.

The diagrams show how properties are used in this dataset. They do not redefine the general meaning of the reused vocabularies. The two regional card-game launches are classified as both CIDOC CRM activities and Schema.org events; this does not establish a subclass relationship between those classes.

A Small Local Vocabulary

The project defines one local property: neh:featuresCharacter. It connects a game work to a fictional character appearing in it.

Its domain is frbroo:F1_Work and its range is crm:E28_Conceptual_Object. In this project, it links Donkey Kong and Mario Bros. to Mario. These declarations classify the resources connected by the property; they are not validation rules.

The property is documented in ontology.ttl. All other classes and properties are reused from existing vocabularies.

06

TEXT WITH STRUCTURE

TEI Encoding

The selected source text is encoded in XML using TEI, a standard for describing texts and their features. The file brings together the source, its annotations and a catalogue of the resources used in the project.

Document Structure

Section Content
teiHeader Document information, source identification and encoding rules
standOff Entity catalogue, supporting sources and explicit relationships
text Selected Wikipedia passages and the references they cite

Comments divide the code into readable sections. The catalogue is kept separate from the source text, allowing additional information to be documented without changing Wikipedia’s wording.

Encoding Examples

Expand a category to inspect its record. Some attributes and notes are omitted for readability; these extracts use the TEI namespace declared in the full file.

Person
<person n="main" xml:id="pers_ShigeruMiyamoto" source="#wiki_source">
  <persName>Shigeru Miyamoto</persName>
  <affiliation ref="#org_Nintendo" from="1977-04" source="#src_N11">Employee</affiliation>
</person>

The record identifies Miyamoto and documents his employment at Nintendo. The date retains month-level precision.

Organisation
<org n="main" xml:id="org_Nintendo" source="#src_N27 #wiki_source">
  <orgName>Nintendo</orgName>
</org>

Nintendo has its own record, separate from the people who founded or worked for the company.

Place
<place n="main" xml:id="place_Kyoto" source="#wiki_source">
  <placeName>Kyoto</placeName>
</place>

Different mentions of Kyoto refer to the same place record.

Video game work
<bibl n="main" xml:id="work_DonkeyKong" source="#src_N02 #src_N07 #src_N21">
  <title>Donkey Kong (1981)</title>
  <date type="first_release" when="1981" source="#src_N29">1981</date>
</bibl>

The game is described as a work; the date identifies its original arcade release.

Console model
<item n="main" xml:id="model_GameBoy" source="#src_N20">
  <label>Game Boy</label>
  <note type="release" source="#src_N20">Japan launch: <date when="1989-04-21">1989-04-21</date>
  </note>
</item>

The record describes a commercial model. Its release note keeps the territory together with the date.

Fictional character
<item n="main" xml:id="char_Mario" source="#wiki_source #src_N01">
  <label>Mario</label>
</item>

Mario is recorded once, allowing different games and names to connect to the same character.

Media franchise
<item n="main" xml:id="franchise_Pokemon" source="#src_P03 #src_P02">
  <label>Pokémon</label>
</item>

This record represents the franchise rather than an individual video game or card game.

Physical card game
<item n="main" xml:id="game_PokemonTCG" source="#src_P03 #src_P04">
  <label>Pokémon Trading Card Game</label>
</item>

A separate record allows the physical card game to be connected to the franchise.

Concept or category
<item n="main" xml:id="type_Hanafuda" source="#wiki_source">
  <label>Hanafuda</label>
</item>

Hanafuda is a category of playing cards, rather than an individual deck.

Supporting event
<event n="support" xml:id="event_MuseumOpening" source="#src_N25 #src_N26" when="2024-10-02">
  <label>Museum opening</label>
</event>

The event has its own date and evidence, distinct from the museum site.

How Classification Works

Several categories share the generic TEI structure item. Their ontology classes are declared separately in listRelation.

<relation active="#model_GameBoy"
          name="rdf:type"
          passive="schema:ProductModel"/>

This classifies Game Boy as a product model. The TEI element organises the record; the classification states what kind of resource it represents.

From a Text Mention to a Relationship

  1. A mention points to a record

    <rs type="fictional_character" ref="#char_Mario">Mario</rs>

  2. The catalogue identifies the resource

    <item n="main" xml:id="char_Mario"
      source="#wiki_source #src_N01">
  <label>Mario</label>
</item>

  3. A relationship connects it to a work

    <relation active="#work_DonkeyKong"
          name="neh:featuresCharacter"
          passive="#char_Mario"/>

The shared identifier connects the text, the catalogue and the relationship. Dates are annotated selectively when they help explain the chosen resources and their history.

Checking the File

The XML was validated against the TEI All schema. Further checks confirmed that identifiers are unique and internal references resolve correctly. The selected passages were compared with the previous transcription to ensure that their wording was preserved.

These checks confirm the technical consistency of the file. Historical claims and modelling choices require a separate review of their supporting evidence.

A NETWORK OF STATEMENTS

From TEI to RDF

The TEI catalogue was exported as RDF using a Python script and the rdflib library. RDF represents information as triples: a subject, a property and an object.

SUBJECTDonkey Kong→PROPERTYfeatures character→OBJECTMario

The script reads the resource URI base and vocabulary prefixes from the TEI header. It extracts the explicit relationships, classifications, labels, references and selected dates. Dated roles and regional console launches are preserved as readable descriptions.

The resulting dataset.ttl contains 330 triples, including all 71 explicit relationship statements from the TEI. External authority references are expressed as rdfs:seeAlso, without automatically asserting that the linked resources are identical.

WHAT THE CONNECTIONS SHOW

Conclusions and Downloads

The project brings together a selected part of Nintendo’s history as connected, documented resources. It shows character continuity through Mario and Jumpman, and supports a comparison of physical card play across different periods.

The result is a structured interpretation, not a complete history of Nintendo. The selection and modelling choices shape what the graphs can show. Technical validation checks the files; it does not replace the assessment of historical evidence.

The RDF export keeps some contextual information as readable descriptions. Future work could represent these details more precisely, extend the source selection and investigate preservation practices using specific evidence.
